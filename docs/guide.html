How to use LivestockBook

A rough walkthrough of the app, from first launch to a finished export. The whole thing works offline, and everything you enter is stored on your own device.

The app has four tabs along the bottom — Records, Animals, Setup and Export — and that is roughly the order you use them in.

1. Set up your farm

Start in the Setup tab. This is where you create the things your animals and records get attached to:

  • Farms — one entry per holding, if you keep animals in more than one place.
  • Paddocks — the fields, pens or sheds animals move between.
  • Groups — any grouping that suits you, such as breeding stock, lambs or a batch you bought together.
  • Medicines & Vaccines — the products you use, so they can be picked from a list when you log a treatment.

You do not have to fill all of this in before you start. Add what you need now and come back to the rest later.

From the Setup tab you can also open your Profile and add your business or farm name and a logo. These appear on the PDFs you export, so it is worth doing once at the start.

2. Add your animals

Go to the Animals tab and add your livestock. Pick the species — cattle, sheep, pigs, goats, chickens, ducks, turkeys, geese, donkeys, horses, buffalo, rabbits, alpacas, llamas, camels or ostriches — then fill in the details you keep track of, such as tag or ID, name, breed, sex, date of birth, acquired date, and the farm, paddock or group it belongs to. You can also add a photo.

Tapping an animal opens its own page with a full timeline: every record logged against it, in date order, so you can see its whole history in one place.

3. Log records

The Records tab is the day-to-day part of the app. Add a record, choose which animal (or animals) it applies to, pick the type, set the date and add any notes.

  • Movement — animals moving between paddocks, farms or off the holding.
  • Weight — weigh-ins over time.
  • Birth and Death.
  • Vaccination and Medication — pulling from the medicines you set up earlier.
  • Health Check.
  • Sale and Purchase.
  • Other — anything that does not fit the list above.

The habit worth building is logging things as they happen rather than trying to reconstruct them later. It only takes a few seconds per record.

4. Filter and find what you need

Once you have a few months of records, use the filter on the Records tab to narrow things down by animal, record type and date range. It is the quickest way to answer questions like which animals were treated last spring, or what has happened in a particular paddock.

5. Export your files

The Export tab turns what you have into a file you can share. Choose the animals, records and date range you want, then export exactly what you need:

  • An animal register — your livestock listed with their key details.
  • A records export — the filtered records themselves.

Both come out as a PDF or a spreadsheet (CSV) file. PDFs carry your farm name and logo, so they are ready to print, email or hand over at an inspection. The file goes through the normal share sheet, so you can save it to Files, iCloud Drive, Google Drive, or send it straight to someone.

Record-keeping requirements differ from country to country, so check what your own local rules ask for. If something they require is missing, let me know and I will look at adding it.

6. Check your reports

Reports give you a breakdown of your livestock and your activity over different time periods — a quick overview of what you have and what has been happening, without going through records one by one.

7. Back up your data

Because your data lives on your device rather than on a server, backups matter. In settings, Backup saves everything — animals, records, farms, paddocks, groups, medicines and your profile — into a single file that you save wherever you like. Restore reads that file back in, which is also how you move your data to a new phone.

It is worth taking a backup every so often, and definitely before changing phones.
